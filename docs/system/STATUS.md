# SIRCUS system manual: status, findings and open questions

This file tracks what is not yet decided. The manual's body marks each of these "not yet specified" and invents no value for it
(`README.md`, Voice). Answer an item and the matching places in the manual get a value; the "Where" column says which
chapters change.

Every "reference model" entry below says what the VM in `sirc-vm` does today. It is evidence, not a design decision, and the
manual does not say it.

## Open questions for you

| ID | Question | Where it appears | What exists today |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| U1 | **Working RAM and system ROM.** *Answered: 128 KB of working RAM (confirmed: 65,536 words, one segment; the examples' program segment is the cartridge, and their scratch segment is the RAM) and a system ROM.* Region Y0 is the RAM, Y1 the ROM. Still open: RAM type and speed (wait states), ROM size, type and speed. | Ch. 1, ch. 3, ch. 5, ch. 7, appendix | The VM maps 64K words at segment 0, read-only, with a 2-cycle latency, and a scratch segment at 1. |
| U2 | **How a program gets in.** *Answered: at reset the CPU fetches its reset vector from the cartridge (the boot overlay maps the cartridge at segment 0). By convention words 0 and 1 of a cartridge hold the address of the system ROM's start-up routine, which sets the system up and jumps to the cartridge program. A game may put its own boot address there.* Still open: the entry point after boot (a header, a fixed address?), what the ROM sets up, and whether the ROM copies the cartridge's own vector table into RAM or writes a default one. | Ch. 3, ch. 7 | The VM loads a binary into the program segment. |
| U3 | **Reset circuit.** *Confirmed:* a resistor and capacitor into two Schmitt-trigger inverters (74LS14) holding `RSTI` for about 100 ms after +5 V is stable, with a reset button that shorts the capacitor. | Ch. 4, ch. 7 | None. |
| U4 | **SIRCAS reset width.** Still unspecified in the SIRCAS manual. Worked around: SIRCAS takes the stretched reset (2 us or more), as the DUART does. SIRCADA needs 6 cycles and takes `RSTO` directly. | Ch. 4 | Open in `docs/audio`. |
| U5 | **Chip selects and segments.** *Answered: one 74LS138 on A23 to A21, one 2M-word region per device. SIRCADA moved from `0x0C` to `0x60` and SIRCAS is at `0x80`. The VM, the examples and both chip manuals now use these.* | Ch. 1, ch. 3, ch. 5, appendix | Done. |
| U6 | ~~Unmapped addresses.~~ **Resolved, as a proposal:** the decoder's spare outputs Y6 and Y7 go to an AND gate that drives `BERR`, so a cycle to `0xC0`-`0xFF` raises a bus fault at once. | Ch. 3, ch. 5 | Done. |
| U7 | ~~Memory protection.~~ **Resolved, as a proposal:** none. No memory controller; `BPER` is held inactive and `PROT` and `BAT` connect to nothing. | Ch. 3, ch. 5 | Done. |
| U8 | **`IRQ1` and `IRQ2`.** *Answered: `IRQ2` is the serial device (confirmed). The manual's new section on the serial interrupt covers the DUART's eight conditions, the level-sensitive behaviour and the wait behind higher levels.* `IRQ1` stays free: the cartridge, if it needs an interrupt. | Ch. 4, ch. 5, appendix | Done for `IRQ2`. |
| U9 | **Pull-up values.** *Worked out:* `BACK` must rise above the input-high level in about a third of a cycle, which for a net of 100 pF needs 1.5 kOhm or less; that needs drivers that sink 3.3 mA, so the DUART drives `BACK` only through its buffer. Still open: the sink capability of SIRCADA and SIRCAS (their manuals give none, see U19), and the values for the interrupt lines. | Ch. 4, ch. 5 | Each device manual says only that the system supplies them. |
| U10 | ~~CPU `SYNC`, `HALT`, `TRCE`.~~ **Resolved, as a proposal:** `HALT` and `TRCE` held inactive by resistors, `SYNC` unconnected. | Ch. 5 | Done. |
| U11 | **Oscillator and clock distribution.** *Confirmed:* a crystal oscillator module at the region's frequency, a D flip-flop for the divide by two (even duty cycle), one `CLKI` net to all three chips, and a separate 3.6864 MHz crystal for the DUART. Still open: tolerance and the electrical characteristics of the clock. | Ch. 2 | Both frequencies are fixed by the video manual. |
| U12 | ~~Clock range of the CPU and SIRCADA.~~ **Closed:** the system runs them at only the two clocks, so no range is needed here. | Ch. 2 | Done. |
| U13 | **Audio memory part.** *Confirmed:* two 32K by 8 static RAMs (the 62256 type), 70 ns or better. Still open: nothing else the manual needs. | Ch. 5 | The SIRCAS manual asks for an access within 2 cycles. |
| U14 | **Converter and analogue stage.** *Converter: the Philips TDA1543 (economy version, Feb 1991), replacing the hi-fi TDA1541A; no `SCK` pin, so `SCK` is unconnected. Output stage: per channel a current-to-voltage stage (680 Ohm and 5.6 nF, 1.56 V full scale about the 2.2 V reference), a second-order Sallen-Key low-pass at 14.4 kHz (10 kOhm, 10 kOhm, 1.5 nF, 820 pF) and a 10 uF coupling capacitor, on half of a quad rail-to-rail op-amp; no bias resistor; about 1.1 V RMS at full scale. You left the stage to me, so it stands as my proposal.* *Connector decided: white and red RCA jacks for the left and right audio (see U15 for the video).* Still open: the op-amp part and the edge timing of `SD` and `WS` (the converter needs 32 ns set-up and 2 ns hold; SIRCAS leaves it open). | Ch. 1, ch. 5, ch. 7 | The datasheet's own circuit has a 1.2 kOhm and 3.3 nF feedback. |
| U15 | **Video output.** *Decided: composite video on a yellow RCA jack, with an optional RF adapter. Encoder: the Motorola MC1377 (datasheet supplied, Motorola 1995; I believe the part is older, which I could not check).* Wired in chapter 5 from the datasheet. Findings: (1) it needs +12 V (10 to 14 V), 35 mA, about 60 mA loaded (U18); (2) its composite output is 2.0 V peak to peak from 50 Ohm, and a 150 Ohm load plus 75 Ohm in series gives the standard 1.0 V into 75 Ohm; the output rests above 4 V so the jack needs a large coupling capacitor; (3) at SIRCADA's 5.4 MHz pixel rate it needs the chroma bandpass transformer and a 400 ns luminance delay line, two costly external parts; *decided: keep both for the best composite picture, since the console is expensive; the cheaper passive network of the datasheet's Fig. 14b would need no delay line but gives more cross-colour*; (4) SIRCADA's CSYNC connects directly (low sync tip, high blanking, pulse 4.7 us inside 2.5 to 5.2 us), and its blanked RGB and serrated vertical sync suit it; (5) its subcarrier comes from a crystal (3.579545 or 4.43361875 MHz), or in NTSC from the master clock divided by 6 into pin 17. Still open: the `IREF` resistor that sets SIRCADA's full scale to 1.0 V, the coupling capacitor value, and the trimmer. | Ch. 1, ch. 5, ch. 7 | The video manual leaves the level open. |
| U16 | **Controller ports.** *Answered and confirmed: shift-register controllers (4021 or 74HC165, up to 16 buttons), read by software through the serial device's parallel pins: `OP2` clock, `OP3` latch, `IP2` and `IP3` data, one pass reading both ports in about 50 accesses (0.2 to 0.4% of a frame). Port 1 is controller only; port 2 is a controller or a TTL serial cable on channel B, sharing pins through open-collector gates; a modem or PC uses an adapter cable with the RS-232 level circuit, so the console needs none.* Still open: the connectors and their pins, the button layout and bit order, and whether the pads carry an id. | Ch. 5, ch. 7 | None in any manual. |
| U17 | **Cartridge connector and the physical form** of the console. *Decided: a 60-contact card edge on 2.54 mm pitch, 30 contacts in each row, in chapter 5 (Table of the cartridge connector). It carries D0 to D15, A0 to A20, `CS` (from Y2, boot overlay behind it), BRW, `IRQ1` (now the cartridge's), `RSTO`, `CLKI`, `CART` (the detect pin of U27), +5 V on four contacts and ground on twelve, one reserved. No `BACK`: the console ends every cartridge access after three cycles. Writes reach the cartridge, so it may hold bank registers or battery RAM; the console budgets 250 mA for a cartridge, raised from 50 mA so a coprocessor such as the Super FX class (RISC chip, own RAM, `IRQ1` for done) fits: I could not find what the SNES allowed, so 250 mA is my estimate. A coprocessor that shares cartridge ROM or RAM with the CPU must arbitrate itself, since there is no `BACK` on the connector.* My choices, for you to change: the pin count and order, `IRQ1` for the cartridge, no `BACK`, `CLKI` and `RSTO` on the connector. Still open: the physical shell, the connector part and its insertion life, the console's total bus loading with a cartridge, and the reserved pin. | Ch. 1, ch. 4, ch. 5, ch. 7 | None. |
| U18 | **Power.** Supply voltages for the CPU, SIRCADA and SIRCAS, regulation, decoupling, the connector, and now **where the +12 V comes from.** | Ch. 7 | Answered for the converter (+5 V only, 50 mA typical, 60 mA maximum), the serial device (+5 V, 150 mA maximum) and the video encoder (+12 V, unregulated is fine, 35 to 60 mA, 600 mW). *Decided: a regulated 12 V adapter, 1 A; the encoder takes the 12 V directly and a step-down switching regulator makes the 5 V (a linear one would dissipate about 8 W). My rough budget is 1.4 A at 5 V, with 150 mA assumed for each custom chip, so it moves when U20 is answered.* Still open: the regulator part, the connector, decoupling. |
| U19 | **Electrical timing between chips.** *Partly answered, in chapter 6 (Electrical Margins). Decided: the decoder is the **74ALS138** (20 ns, same pins, common and cheap by 1993), a glue gate is 15 ns and the board 5 to 10 ns; the figures below were first worked with the 74LS138 at 40 ns and the margins are now 20 ns larger. Target for the chips, which are mine to design: outputs valid within 25 ns of the rising `CLKI` edge, inputs need 10 ns of set-up and no hold (my guess from late-1980s CMOS CPUs, now written into the CPU hardware manual's electrical chapter as provisional targets, with the CLKI range 9 to 12 MHz, a 3 ns output hold and TTL levels; leaves 41 ns on the memory paths and 33 ns on one-cycle devices).* Findings: (1) with 100 ns working memory and ROM only 26 ns is left for the CPU's output delay and set-up, and with a 200 ns cartridge only 14 ns, so I changed the parts to **70 ns** for working memory and ROM and **150 ns** for the cartridge (56, 56 and 64 ns left). A faster decoder, the 74ALS138 or 74F138, would add 20 ns. (2) The write strobe must end at the falling edge of `CLKI` in the last cycle, not when `BAS` rises, or the SRAM's data hold fails; the strobe is then 95 ns (2 cycles) or 190 ns (3 cycles). (3) `BAS` stays low across the two words of a fetch, so the 74LS164 wait-state generator must restart after each `BACK`; the manual now says so. (4) My earlier pull-up (1.5 kOhm, a rise within a third of a cycle) did not match its own arithmetic; the rise has about two thirds of a cycle (60 ns), which needs **1.2 kOhm or less** and 3.8 mA sinks (U9). **The 74LS138 delay is checked against the National datasheet you supplied (June 1989): 40 ns worst case at 50 pF, 27 to 28 ns at 15 pF; the 74ALS138's 20 ns is still from my memory.** Still open: the CPU's output delay, set-up and hold; SIRCADA's and SIRCAS's, which should meet: CPU out plus set-up of 56 ns or less on the memory paths, a one-cycle device's CS-to-data and CS-to-`BACK` of 25 ns or less, and SIRCADA's clock-to-address plus data set-up of 53 ns or less; the DUART's `DTACK` timing; the decoder part. | Ch. 5, ch. 6 | None. |
| U20 | **SIRCAS and SIRCADA package and pin numbers.** SIRCAS is open in its manual. SIRCADA was a 52-pin PLCC, but with its memory interface it has 79 signals and its manual no longer fixes a package (U32). Either would need a package larger than 84 pins. Chapter 5 shows both by name only. | Ch. 1, ch. 5 | Open in `docs/audio` and `docs/video`. |
| U21 | ~~Region parts.~~ **Closed:** a PAL console is a PAL SIRCADA and a PAL oscillator; SIRCAS has no variant. | Ch. 2 | Done. |
| U22 | ~~The 16-bit word in a 24-bit slot.~~ **Resolved:** SIRCAS now sends 16-bit slots, 32 bit clocks a frame of 10 and 11 `CLKI` cycles alternately, so nothing follows the 16th bit (`docs/audio`, serial output; the model and its test are changed). | Ch. 2, ch. 5 | Done. |
| U23 | **Installing interrupt handlers.** *Answered: a boot overlay. Your idea of mapping the cartridge at segment 0 is in the manual.* While the overlay is set the cartridge answers at segment 0 (the system ROM if no cartridge is present) and the RAM does not. `RSTO` sets it. A write to the system ROM's own segment clears it, after which RAM answers at segment 0. Words 0 and 1 of the cartridge are the reset vector. A game can clear the overlay itself with the same write, so it can do without the ROM. Still open: the segments of the ROM and cartridge, and confirmation that the clearing trigger is a write to the ROM segment (no extra address needed) and not a register. | Ch. 3, ch. 4, ch. 7 | None. |
| U24 | **System ROM driver routines.** Where is the entry table, what is the calling convention, which services (video set-up, loading the audio memory, input, and so on)? Probably a manual of its own. | Ch. 3 (only mentions them) | Nothing yet. |
| U25 | ~~BACK for the memories, and wait states.~~ **Resolved, as a proposal:** a 74LS164 wait-state generator. Working memory and system ROM one wait state (2 cycles, parts of 70 ns or better, revised from 100 ns under U19); cartridge two (3 cycles, 150 ns or better, revised from 200 ns). **Consequence:** an ordinary instruction takes 8 cycles from RAM or ROM and 10 from the cartridge, so a frame holds 22,336 (RAM) or 17,868 (cartridge) instructions in NTSC, against 29,781 with none. Zero wait states needs a faster decoder (74F138) and memory of 25 ns or less (revised from 55 ns under U19), memory. Chapter 6 has both tables. The "part needed" column is conservative: it gives the memory the whole cycle less the decoder delay and the CPU's set-up time. Once the CPU's address-valid and data-sample times are specified (U19) it can probably be relaxed, since consoles of the era gave a ROM roughly 40 to 50% of the cycle. | Ch. 3, ch. 5, ch. 6 | Done. |
| U26 | ~~How the chip selects are made.~~ **Resolved: one 74LS138** (see U5). Spare outputs Y6 and Y7 can drive `BERR` for unmapped regions (U6). The overlay needs a 74LS74, clocked by `CLKI` so a decode glitch cannot clear it, and a few gates: Y0 is gated by the overlay (RAM when clear, cartridge or ROM when set). | Ch. 3, ch. 5 | Done. |
| U27 | ~~No cartridge.~~ **Resolved:** fall back to the ROM. A cartridge grounds a pin on its connector, with a pull-up on the console; the overlay maps the ROM at segment 0 while the pin is high. By default the ROM shows the SIRCUS logo and a missing-cartridge icon; holding a controller button at reset enters a simple monitor that loads a program over the serial port (U30). | Ch. 3, ch. 7 | Done. |
| U28 | **The serial I/O device: MC68681 DUART.** *Answered: pins, register addresses, bus timing, the crystal, the reset and the glue are in chapter 5, from the original Motorola advance information (the scan you supplied). The 1996 manual is the CMOS MC68HC681, a pin-compatible later part, used only to cross-check.* Findings: (1) `RESET` needs 1.0 us and `RSTO` gives 559 ns, so a pulse stretcher is needed (U29). (2) `DTACK` follows the DUART's own clock, not `CLKI`, so a cycle takes 2 to 9 cycles and `BACK` is re-timed by a flip-flop and an open-collector gate. (3) 3.6864 MHz crystal; deriving it from the master clock would be 3% off at NTSC and 4% at PAL. (4) Its outputs sink 2.4 mA at 0.4 V, which limits the pull-ups (U9). (5) It draws up to 150 mA (U18). (6) The original's timing figure draws `RESET` as a high pulse, but its table and the 1996 manual say active low; I followed those. Still open: the connectors, the handshake lines, the speed range, which of `IRQ1` and `IRQ2`, and the RS-232 level circuit or an all-TTL design (U16). | Ch. 3, ch. 5, ch. 7 | The VM has a `TERMINAL` device at `0x0A` and a `DEBUG` device at `0x0B`; you said not to treat them as the source of truth. They fall inside the RAM region in the new map. |
| U29 | ~~Reset pulse stretcher.~~ **Resolved, as a proposal:** a 74LS123 monostable on the fall of `RSTO`, 2 us or more, feeding the DUART and SIRCAS. The start-up routine waits 3 us before touching them. | Ch. 4, ch. 5 | Done. |

| U32 | **SIRCADA's external video memory.** *Decided, and kept: two 32K by 8 static RAMs, like the audio memory. The cost is accepted, and is recorded in `DESIGN-NOTES.md` as one reason SIRCUS was expensive. In the SIRCADA manual: a memory interface (MA0 to MA14, MD0 to MD15, MCE, MOE, MWE), 79 signals so the package is unspecified, a CPU access to video memory takes two cycles (one wait state), registers, palette and sprite table stay at one cycle, and a `DMAT` of 255 words into video memory takes 777 cycles, not 522.* The system manual proposes 35 ns or better, because the renderer reads one word every 93 ns. Still open: the part and its cost (35 ns SRAM was cache-class and dearer than the 70 ns audio memory), the interface set-up and hold times (U19) and the package. **The VM does not model the extra cycle:** its video device answers VRAM accesses in one cycle, so it needs a VRAM access latency of two cycles to follow the manual. | Ch. 1, ch. 3, ch. 5 | The VM treats video memory as internal. |
| U33 | **The optional RF adapter.** Not part of the console. It would take composite video and the two audio channels and modulate them onto a television channel, mixing the audio to mono. | U34 | **An RGB output as well.** *Decided: yes.* Chapter 5 adds four 75 Ohm buffers (gain two, output to ground, 20 MHz or better) taken from SIRCADA's R, G, B and `CSYNC` ahead of the encoder, and chapter 7 a nine-pin multi-out connector with R, G, B, composite sync, audio left and right, composite video and two grounds. The picture needs neither chroma network nor delay line. Still open: the connector type (a cable to SCART or a monitor is the only accessory), the sync level (I gave 1 V into 75 Ohm), the amplifier part, and SIRCADA's output source impedance, which the encoder and the buffers now share (U19). | Ch. 1, ch. 5, ch. 7 | Nothing. |

## Findings while writing

* **The PAL clock is already fixed.** The SIRCADA manual gives a PAL master clock of 21,281,370 Hz (the PAL subcarrier times
  4.8). With the divide by two, `CLKI` is 10,640,685 Hz, which is inside SIRCAS's 9.0 to 12.0 MHz range and makes every note
  15.9 cents flat of NTSC. The SIRCAS manual still says "the frequency of `CLKI` in a PAL system is not yet specified"
  (`docs/audio/chapters/01b-package-and-pinout.tex`) and lists the PAL clock as open in `README.md`. **Done at your request**: the pinout
  chapter, the clock-rate and timing sections, the device summary, `README.md` and `STATUS.md` of `docs/audio` now give
  10,640,685 Hz.
* **The converter is now the TDA1543 (1991), not the TDA1541A.** The economy part needs only +5 V and takes standard I2S; see `DESIGN-NOTES.md`.
* **Earlier note: the converter datasheet supplied was the TDA1541A (1991), not the TDA1541 (1985) I had read before.** The 1985 text needed an `SCK` input; the 1991 part runs its own oscillator and has none. The SIRCAS notes are corrected (`docs/audio/STATUS.md`, Target DAC).
* **Frame rate rounding.** The video manual quotes 50.00 Hz for PAL. At 212,784 cycles a frame and 10,640,685 Hz it is
  50.007 Hz, so this manual says 50.01 Hz. NTSC is 60.10 Hz in both.
* **Video line length.** The video manual uses 1364 master clocks a line (60.10 Hz frames). A broadcast NTSC line is 1365.0
  clocks, so the video runs 0.07% fast against broadcast. This does not affect any figure here; noted in case you want a
  broadcast-exact frame.
* **Devices that exist only in the VM.** `TERMINAL` at segment `0x0A` and `DEBUG` at `0x0B` are simulator devices. They are
  not part of the console, so the manual does not mention them. If you want them reserved on the real map, say so.

## Choices I made in the draft

All easy to change.

| Choice | Why |
| --- | --- |
| The serial part is the original NMOS MC68681, not the CMOS MC68HC681 | The era is 1993 (`DESIGN-NOTES.md`) and the NMOS part dates from 1985. The CMOS part is a drop-in. |
| `DTACK` is re-timed by a flip-flop on `CLKI` | `DTACK` follows the DUART's own clock. Without it the CPU could sample `BACK` at a bad moment. |
| The serial device is in region Y5 (`0xA0`) | It is the next free output of the decoder. Y6 and Y7 stay free. |
| One 2M-word region per device | You said space is not a constraint, and it needs one decoder. |
| Selects: 74LS138 named, layout open | You chose the 74LS138. The body says the logic is made of 74LS138 decoders and gives the segments that are fixed (U26). |
| Cartridge at the low words at reset | Your proposal: the overlay maps the cartridge at segment 0, and its words 0 and 1 point at the ROM start-up routine. |
| The manual says SIRCAS's segment is unspecified, not `0x0D` | The SIRCAS manual says so. The VM's `0x0D` is only evidence (U5). |
| Both regions given in every table | The PAL clock is fixed by the video manual, so there is nothing to withhold. |
| Block diagram has one clock rail and no interrupt lines | The lines are in ch. 4 and the net tables of ch. 5. |
| `BACK` drawn as a shared open-drain net | Both devices' manuals say it is open drain. Whether the program memory takes part is open (U1). |
